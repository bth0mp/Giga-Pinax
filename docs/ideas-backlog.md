## Other projects to plug in

ANS runs several type corpora on the same Numishare software as OCRE and PELLA, with the same Atom search and JSON-LD records, so each is mostly a catalogue option plus a URL:

1. ~~**CRRO**~~ — shipped in v0.4 (see Done). Crawford RRC references. *Probed 2026-09-10:* `numismatics.org/crro/apis/search?q=RRC 44/5` returns title `RRC 44/5`, id `rrc-44.5`, CORS open — exact-title matching works as-is, so this is a third catalogue option plus a query rule (`RRC {number}`).
2. ~~**SCO / Seleucid Coins Online**~~ shipped in v0.10 (see Done). **PCO / Ptolemaic Coins Online** remains. *Probed:* both CORS open, but titles differ from the collector's shorthand — SCO `Seleucid Coins (part 1) 1266.2` (id `sc.1.1266.2`), PCO indexes CPE not Svoronos (`Coins of the Ptolemaic Empire Vol. I, Part II, no. B549`, id `cpe.1_2.B549`). Needs a per-corpus title builder, and PCO needs a Svoronos→CPE story before it helps. *SCO update 2026-09-11:* every record lives at `sco/id/sc.1.{number}` whatever the part (`sc.1.1266.2`, `sc.1.1630.2b`, `sc.1.1`, `sc.1.1439`, `sc.1.1440` all 200), so an `SC {number}` catalogue can fetch the record directly with `lookupById('sco', 'sc.1.' + number)` — no part mapping; the quoted title only matches with the exact `(part N)`, and the plain search `SC 1266.2` already ranks the right type first as a fallback.
3. **HRC** (Hellenistic Royal Coinages) and **Coins of the Roman Empire** extensions as coverage grows. *Probed 2026-09-11:* HRC's search aggregates PELLA (`price.L46`, Lysimachus) and SCO (`sc.1.293`) records plus its own series — notably **Le Rider's Philip II** corpus (titles `LeRider Philip II 5.86`, ids `lerider.philip_ii.5.86`; 2,472 hits for "Philip II"), CORS open. A "Le Rider" catalogue for Philip II would be the next new coverage; check whether its ids are predictable like SCO's before choosing search-first or record-first. *Probed again 2026-09-11:* the Le Rider records actually live in **PELLA** (`numismatics.org/pella/id/lerider.philip_ii.1.1.jsonld`; the `hrc/id/…` URLs answer 404) and are **die-combination entries**, not types — `LeRider Philip II 1.1` is obverse die D1 / reverse die R1, with `skos:broader` → the type `pella.philip_ii.1` ("PELLA Philip II 1"); other titles are plate figures (`LeRider Philip II Pl. 43, Fig. 3`). The first number is a group (titles sampled: groups 1–5). A collector's "Le Rider 86" can't be mapped to these ids without knowing their citation style, and die-level prices aren't meaningful — ask the collector how they cite Philip II coins before building anything; PELLA's own `Philip II {n}` type series may be the better target.
4. **RPC Online** (Oxford, Roman Provincial Coinage) — provincial references; separate site and API, check its terms. *Probed 2026-09-11:* every URL, including the home page, answers `403 Forbidden` to a plain scripted request — bot protection, not a public API. Don't work around it; ask the RPC team about an approved integration (as with acsearch) before planning anything.
5. **Specimen thumbnails** from OCRE/PELLA example coins (IIIF images from ANS, BM, BnF) in the type card.
6. **Numista** — has an official API with a key; natural backbone for the **Giga Collection** tracker (your coins, purchase price vs current median, CSV export).
7. **Upcoming-sale alerts** (NumisBids, Sixbid, Biddr, CoinArchives) — "this type is in an auction next week". Each needs its own permission check before any fetch.

- v0.23 **a book, a sale and a scholar's name are not references, and the ones written oddly now read**: four fixes in `lot.js`, all from the round-2 coverage audit. (1) A publication year after a key is no longer a type number when the line says it is a book: a typed catalogue whose numbers never reach it drops it outright (Crawford's Republic ends in the 500s, a Bopearachchi series is one or two digits, so `Crawford 1974` and `Bopearachchi 1991` no longer send a wrong request to numismatics.org), and every other key needs explicit evidence in the same clause — a citation cue in front, in the same sentence (`see`, `following`, `compare`, `published`, `discussed`, `dated`; `by`, `in` and `as` only where they tail one of those, since a dealer writes them of the coin itself — `Same obverse die as Price 1533` and `As RIC 1600, but with a star` are the coin, not a book; a leading `cf.` is read as part of the reference and never as a book citation) or a page, note, edition, bracketed year, verb of argument or a bibliography's bare plate behind — but not a bare colon or a note number written after a comma, which is the dealer's own aside (`RIC 1600: this coin`, `RIC 1600, no. 5`) — (`Price 1991, p. 123`, `Sear 2000 lists this`, `Svoronos 1904 pl. 12`; a plate hung on the number with a comma, `Svoronos 1600, pl. 20`, is part of the citation, and a hyphenated pair of numbers after it is a reign date, not a page range). With no evidence the reference is kept, because Price runs past 3900 and Sear, Hendin, Svoronos and SNG Copenhagen all have real numbers in the 1500-2100 range, and losing one of those costs more than a stray prices-only row. (2) A house sale is not a catalogue: a house key whose number is followed by `, lot 1234` is a sale (`Album 46, lot 1234`, `Sear 25, lot 12`), though a catalogue keeps its own number in front of one (`RIC 972, lot 123`); a name key after a word and `&` or `and` is part of a firm's name (`Aureo & Calico 300`, `Freeman & Sear 15`), while the same words after a number join two references and both are listed (`RIC 972 and Cohen 17`); and a name key after a forename's initial is the person (`David R. Sear certificate`), never a regnal or volume numeral (`Philip I. RIC 12`, `RPC I. Sear 12`) and never a lower-case letter, which is a weight or a die axis (`17.21 g, 8 h. Sear 2537`). `Price realized`/`realised` is plain English. (3) One or two short words from a closed list may now sit between a key and its number: an edition or volume word is read past (`Hendin 6th ed. 1243` is Hendin 1243, `Hunter, vol.
